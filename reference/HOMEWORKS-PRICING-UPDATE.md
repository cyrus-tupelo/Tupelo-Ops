# Homeworks Published Pricing — Standing Update Procedure

## Why a dated snapshot of a web page, and why it needs more care than the other sources

Every other source in `reference/` is an **export**: Relay statements, Gusto payroll journals, Stripe balance history, the Homeworks invoice PDF and catalog CSV. An export is generated by the system of record, arrives with a coverage window, and can be gated — `parse_invoices.py` and `reconcile_payroll_relay.py` both fail closed when their input doesn't reconcile.

This source is none of those things. It is a **live vendor marketing page**, transcribed by hand. That creates three failure modes the other procedures don't have to handle:

1. **Silent staleness.** A price change produces no diff, no error, and no signal of any kind. The snapshot keeps looking authoritative while being wrong. There is no gate that can catch this — only re-reading the page.
2. **No supersession guarantee.** A newer snapshot is not necessarily a superset of an older one. A tier can be renamed, merged, or withdrawn, and the figure the business actually contracted under may exist only in an older snapshot. This is the same reasoning `CATALOG-UPDATE.md` gives for using an explicit pointer rather than glob-latest — it applies here with more force, because pricing history is contractually meaningful.
3. **Transcription risk.** There is no machine-readable original to diff against. The mitigation is the arithmetic self-check block in each snapshot (cadence deltas must reconcile against the vendor's own stated annual-savings figures) — that check is what catches a mis-typed price.

Consequently: **never overwrite or edit a prior snapshot.** Each refresh is a new dated file, and the pointer moves.

## Steps

1. **Open `https://www.home.works/pricing`** in a browser (or fetch it). Read the full page, including any comparison table and any fine print.

2. **Save a new snapshot** as `reference/homeworks-pricing-YYYY-MM-DD.md`, dated the day it was read. Copy the structure of the existing snapshot: source URL, fetch date, the tier table, a verbatim-quotes section, the arithmetic self-check, the "Stated nowhere on this page" section, and the relationship-to-other-files section. **Never edit a prior snapshot** — `reference/` is append-only ground truth (`reference/README.md`).

3. **Run the arithmetic self-check before trusting the transcription.** Confirm each tier's `monthly − annual_effective` times 12 equals the vendor's own stated annual saving. If they disagree, you have mis-read one of the two figures — resolve it against the page before continuing. Also recompute the two plan gaps and the included-seat advantage.

4. **Re-check the "Stated nowhere on this page" list.** Each refresh, actively look for the six absent items again — especially **Enterprise's SMS overage rate** and **whether Field Force users consume a seat**, which are the two gaps that most affect `CONTEXT.md` Follow-Up #30's math. If the page now states one, move it out of that section into the tier table and update the corresponding `model/data/assumptions.csv` row from `BLOCKED` to `REFERENCE`.

5. **Update the pointer** in `reference/README.md`'s "Homeworks published pricing" section:

   ```
   **Active pricing snapshot: `homeworks-pricing-YYYY-MM-DD.md`**
   ```

   > **Do not write `**Active snapshot:`** here.** That exact string is the *service catalog's* pointer, and `model/parse_invoices.py:118` regex-searches the whole of `reference/README.md` for it and takes the **first** match. A second `**Active snapshot:**` line placed above the catalog's would silently redirect the invoice parser to this pricing file and break the revenue pipeline. The distinct `**Active pricing snapshot:**` label cannot match that regex.

6. **Reconcile the `REFERENCE` rows** in `model/data/assumptions.csv` against the new snapshot. Every row whose `source` field cites a pricing snapshot must be checked, and its `source` updated to name the new snapshot file. These are a **linked pair with this file, not two facts that happen to agree** — the same discipline `CLAUDE.md`'s assumption-cell sync rule applies to TARGET/POLICY rows.

   Also check `reference/fixed-overhead.md`'s Homeworks row and `CONTEXT.md` Follow-Up #30 for any figure that moved.

7. **Re-run the analysis and the workbook:**

   ```
   python model/homeworks_plan_analysis.py
   python model/build_model.py
   ```

   The analysis script reads every constant from `assumptions.csv` — if a price changed, its thresholds move automatically. No ledger is touched, so a full `refresh_all.py` run is not required for a pricing-only refresh.

8. **Review `git diff`** on `model/data/assumptions.csv` and the new snapshot. The diff is the period's pricing report.

9. **Log to `HISTORY.md`** — one entry naming what changed on the page, which `assumptions.csv` rows moved, and whether any of the six unknowns got answered.

10. **Commit.**

## Cadence

There is **no fixed schedule and no correctness gate** — nothing in the pipeline breaks if this goes stale, which is exactly why it needs a named trigger instead. Refresh:

- **Whenever `CONTEXT.md` Follow-Up #30 is actually acted on.** This is the binding one. A plan-tier decision must never be made against a snapshot that wasn't re-read the same session. SaaS pricing changes without notice.
- At each Revision Point, as part of the standing staleness review.
- Whenever the owner notices a billing amount that doesn't match the snapshot — that is direct evidence of a price change and takes priority over the schedule.

## What a refresh produces and gates on

**No fail-closed gate (deliberate, and unlike Relay/payroll).** Nothing downstream halts on a stale pricing snapshot, because there is no way to detect staleness from inside the repo. The arithmetic self-check in step 3 is a *transcription* check, not a currency check — it proves the figures are internally consistent, not that they are current. The only real control is step 5's binding cadence trigger.

**Known standing limitation.** The page publishes list prices only. It cannot show a negotiated or grandfathered rate. The business's actual Homeworks cost is therefore always taken from `model/data/ledger-overhead.csv`, never from this source — the snapshot is authoritative for *tiers the business is not on* (i.e. Enterprise, and the annual option) and for structural facts (seat counts, contact caps, fee rates), not for what the business currently pays.
